## chatgpt-0003

Plan the four weeks around two firings: one bisque firing and one glaze firing.

### Week by week

| Week | In class | Kiln work after class |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Make two mugs or bowls each. Short talk on clay and drying. | None. Pieces dry for a week. |
| 2 | Trim and add handles. | Bisque firing of all pieces. |
| 3 | Glaze the bisqued pieces. Test tiles first. | Glaze firing. |
| 4 | Pick up the pieces and talk about what each glaze did. | None. |

### Why this order

- Greenware must be bone dry before the bisque firing, so week 1 work cannot be fired the same week.
- The glaze firing must finish and cool before week 4, so it has to start no later than the day after week 3.
- Two pieces per person keeps the load small enough for one kiln.

### What to check

1. How big your kiln is, so we know whether 16 pieces fit in one load.
2. How long your glaze firing takes, including cooling.
3. Whether you want test tiles in the same load.

Tell me the kiln size and firing time and I'll fix the exact days.

## chatgpt-0006

how much clay do I need per person?

## chatgpt-0007

For two mugs or small bowls each:

- About 1.5 lb of clay per finished piece, plus a third more for practice and trimming.
- That is about 4 lb per person, or 32 lb for eight people.
- Buy two 25 lb bags. The extra covers a failed piece or a ninth student.

Use one clay body for the whole class, so every piece fires at the same temperature. A mid-fire stoneware is the usual choice for an electric kiln.
